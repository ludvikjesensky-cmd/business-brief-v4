# Fast Editorial — Reconstructed Workflow

**Status:** GREENFIELD WORKING SPEC  
**Source basis:** original v3 experiment Chat reconstruction  
**Canonical:** not yet  
**Scope:** mechanism only; no later editorial constitution added yet

---

## 1. Core operating model

Fast Editorial is one continuous editorial Chat working through one full day.

The reconstructed sequence is:

```
Complete edition A
→ independent Issue Map
→ freeze

Complete edition B
→ independent Issue Map
→ freeze

Complete edition C
→ independent Issue Map
→ freeze

Three frozen Issue Maps
→ Cross-Paper
→ Daily Topics

Selected Daily Topics
→ Selective Deep Read
→ corrected / qualified Topics

Editorial Review
→ 8 passes
→ final quality gates
→ verified Master Brief
```

The order of FT, WSJ and Handelsblatt is not fixed. What matters is the actual intake order and independent treatment of each edition.

The same Chat should normally hold the whole process because live context is part of the method.

---

## 2. Intake and source readiness

For every edition record:

- editorial day;
- publication;
- edition date;
- source reference;
- intake order;
- page count;
- text-layer usability;
- reading mode;
- important limitations.

The process starts from a **complete edition**, not a headline feed or article shortlist.

If the edition is image-based, visual reading is acceptable. Full-issue OCR is not automatically required.

Do not pretend that unreadable or unavailable material was reviewed.

---

## 3. Independent issue reading

Read the whole edition before Cross-Paper.

During this stage, the unit of work is the **article / editorial object inside one publication**.

Distinguish:
- article;
- continuation;
- teaser;
- analysis;
- opinion;
- interview;
- advertisement / partner content;
- market tables and other non-editorial material.

Do not prematurely collapse many articles into broad buckets such as “AI”, “rates” or “oil”.

For every relevant object capture:
- source location;
- headline / working label;
- genre;
- article point;
- editorial value;
- priority;
- open questions;
- preliminary Deep Read need;
- structural notes.

Priority is the judgment **inside that edition**, not a promise of placement in the final Brief.

---

## 4. Freeze

After the whole edition has been reviewed:

1. complete the Issue Map;
2. write the issue-level conclusion;
3. record limitations and process caveats;
4. mark the map `frozen`.

Freeze means:

- later newspapers may change the item's relevance to the day;
- later newspapers must not silently rewrite the original independent judgment of the edition.

If an objective error is later discovered, preserve the original snapshot and record the correction explicitly.

---

## 5. Second and third editions

Repeat the same process independently.

The Chat may remember earlier editions, but that memory must not become the filter for the new map.

Do not:
- skip an article because another newspaper already covered it;
- promote an article merely because another title also covered it;
- begin Cross-Paper before all three Issue Maps are frozen.

Editorial independence is procedural, not amnesia.

---

## 6. Cross-Paper

Only after all three maps are frozen does the unit of work change:

```
article inside a publication
→ daily story supported by one or more articles
```

Create Daily Topics by asking whether articles jointly explain a sufficiently concrete:

- event;
- mechanism;
- problem;
- tension;
- strategic question.

Do not merge by keyword, sector or company name alone.

A strong single-source story may remain INCLUDE. Source count is not a quality score.

### Apparent disagreement test

Before calling two sources contradictory, check:

1. time / market snapshot;
2. metric;
3. segment;
4. geography;
5. period;
6. fact vs forecast vs opinion.

Different source descriptions may both be correct if they describe different slices of reality.

### Source contribution roles

Use:
- primary;
- supporting;
- contrasting;
- background.

These roles describe what a source contributes, not its prestige.

### Decisions

Each Daily Topic receives:
- INCLUDE;
- RESERVE;
- EXCLUDE.

RESERVE is not a trash bin. It may later serve:
- short coverage;
- curation;
- Detail;
- replacement if the hierarchy changes.

Cross-Paper produces a daily story map, not the final shape of the reader-facing Brief.

---

## 7. Selective Deep Read

Selective Deep Read is a distinct step between Cross-Paper and Editorial Review.

Its purpose is not merely to fact-check known doubts.

It must perform two jobs:

### 7.1 Resolve known uncertainty

Examples:
- monthly vs year-to-date;
- intraday vs close;
- negotiation vs agreement;
- possibility vs decision;
- fact vs opinion;
- metric / denominator / segment.

### 7.2 Discover unknown problems

Return to source even when the current synthesis appears convincing.

Question:
> Is our interpretation actually supported by the source combination?

Deep Read may:
- narrow a claim;
- split a mechanism;
- explain apparent conflict;
- change the working title;
- change the synthesis;
- change selection status;
- establish wording guardrails.

The documented Day 2 process Deep Read all 12 INCLUDE Topics.

This is evidence of method, not a permanent quota.

### Deep Read vs Verification

- **Selective Deep Read** checks whether the editor understands the source story correctly.
- **Verification** later checks whether the concrete words written in the Brief are correct.

These are different controls.

---

## 8. Editorial Review Pipeline

After the selected Daily Topics are sufficiently understood, perform eight editorial passes.

They are eight perspectives of the same editorial process, not necessarily separate agents or Chats.

### Pass 1 — Assignment Editor

Question:
> What belongs in this edition, and what editorial job should each topic perform?

May:
- promote / demote;
- use RESERVE;
- decide lead / main story / short coverage / curation / omission.

Must not treat every INCLUDE as an equally large story.

### Pass 2 — Dramaturg

Question:
> How should the edition work as a whole?

Controls:
- hierarchy;
- order;
- grouping;
- pace;
- reader load.

May combine several Daily Topics into one final editorial unit if the combination preserves factual and conceptual distinctions.

### Pass 3 — Story Editor

Question:
> What is the actual story here?

Defines:
- point;
- today's change;
- mechanism;
- tension;
- reader takeaway.

Must not invent drama unsupported by sources.

### Pass 4 — Brief Writer

Writes the full reader-facing draft.

Rules:
- write natural Czech;
- use variable item lengths;
- preserve hierarchy;
- do not add unsupported general knowledge;
- do not produce a row of equally shaped mini-articles.

### Pass 5 — Detail Editor

Question:
> Has the Brief delivered the point while leaving legitimate value for deeper reading?

Must avoid both:
- hollow teaser;
- full consumption of all deeper argument and evidence.

This pass does not itself create the full Detail product.

### Pass 6 — Verification Editor

Check the actual written claims.

Re-check:
- figures;
- time relationships;
- scope;
- attribution;
- headlines;
- watchlist;
- implications;
- certainty.

Correct understanding during Deep Read does not guarantee accurate compression during writing.

### Pass 7 — Language & Style Editor

Optimize:
- natural Czech;
- rhythm;
- transitions;
- jargon;
- repetition;
- read-aloud quality.

Do not alter factual scope or certainty.

### Pass 8 — Editor-in-Chief

Judge the entire edition as a product.

May:
- approve unchanged;
- cut;
- reorder;
- return a problem to the relevant earlier pass.

A pass is not required to make a change.

“Pass with no change” is valid if the review was genuinely performed.

---

## 9. Final quality gates

Before marking the Brief verified, evaluate:

### Coverage
Has an important part of the day disappeared?

Coverage is a safety net, not a requirement to include every mapped object.

### Hierarchy
Is it obvious what is central and what is secondary?

Hierarchy is expressed through:
- placement;
- space;
- explanatory intensity.

### Curiosity
Does the Brief create legitimate desire to understand more without withholding the core point?

### Trust
Are claims:
- accurate;
- proportionate;
- correctly attributed;
- no stronger than the evidence?

### Product fit
Does the result function as a Business Brief, rather than:
- a pile of article summaries;
- an analyst memo;
- a teaser;
- a translated index?

### 90-second test
Can a fast reader obtain a real map of the day quickly?

This does not mean the whole Brief must be readable in 90 seconds.

---

## 10. Completion

A day is complete when:

- all three Issue Maps are frozen;
- Cross-Paper is complete;
- selected Topics have sufficient Deep Read;
- all eight Editorial Review passes have been performed;
- final gates pass;
- the Master Brief is stored as a versioned canonical text;
- unresolved limitations are explicit.

Status `verified` means editorially approved.

It does not automatically mean:
- published;
- emailed;
- posted on the web;
- full Details created.

---

## 11. Cross-day continuity

Previous-day context is useful, but must not influence independent Issue Maps before freeze.

After freeze it can help distinguish:
- new;
- continuing;
- changed;
- repetitive;
- newly important.

Useful lightweight inputs:
- previous verified Brief;
- open story threads;
- last known state;
- important qualifications;
- source references.

Do not infer a trend merely because a topic recurs.

---

This document does not yet define:

- database implementation;
- automation;

- integration with Deep / Memory.
